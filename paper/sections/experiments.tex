\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate representation necessity, certification efficiency, independent
proposal generation, and complete-schedule quality, keeping finite-evidence
guarantees separate from empirical generalization.

\subsection{Workloads and Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol}
Table~\ref{tab:study-protocol} describes the disjoint split allocation.
Each pair preserves contacts, rewards, intervals, and boundaries while
changing one resource parameter. Temporal regimes use satellite/ground
populations $(8,6)$, $(12,3)$, and $(3,12)$ for balanced, ground-scarce,
and satellite-scarce resources. Ground-gap pairs change from $(0,2)$ in

C3 uses frozen source predicates, successive days for the three splits,
and time-ordered blocks with disjoint original identities. The allocation
contains 27,776 opportunities from a 69,923-opportunity source; conclusions
concern these subproblems. Synthetic seeds and days are disjoint across

Programs and controls are frozen before test access. Quality is schedule
reward divided by the available HiGHS floating MILP upper reference under

cluster shared temporal seeds across regimes, and resample entire C3

\subsection{Certification and Representation Necessity}
\label{sec:experiments:evidence}
The acquisition study runs three conditions on 62 training pairs of at
most 128 contacts: 20 diagnostic and 42 temporal/C3. Ranked and uniform
challengers are both anchored at actual weight-greedy rollout actions;
uniform also permutes query order. Replayed boundaries must remain feasible,
with both competing actions feasible and adjacent in both configurations.
Budgets allow four attempts, two saved relations, 40 conditional calls,
8,000 expanded nodes, and two rollout steps per pair; each call allows
500 nodes and region size 128, with objective tolerance $10^{-8}$.

Clique envelopes increase ranked-query yield from 36 to 51 certificates
while reducing expanded nodes (Table~\ref{tab:evidence-yield}). Ranked
challengers find more reversals than uniform challengers, but fewer total
certificates. Of the 51 ranked/clique specifications, 32 are diagnostic
(24 reversals, 8 preservations); temporal/C3 contribute 2 reversals and
17 preservations. Repeated boundary certificates are not independent samples.

All 64 strict self-loop requirements arise from diagnostic probes. The
combined temporal/C3 quotient has 60 classes, 30 arcs, and no cycle or
self-loop: observed impossibility is confined to constructed aliases.
Preference reversals alone do not establish representation necessity.
Over four structural features, exact enumeration selects neighborhood-edge
count alone, additive standalone work 6,181, and removes the observed
cycles. This minimum concerns that catalogue and fixed evidence. The
four-cycle fixture further shows that covering a coarse witness can leave
a refined cycle; a second full-quotient master repairs it.

\subsection{Independent Proposals and Joint Selection}
\label{sec:experiments:discovery}
Guided joint, free joint, and rule-only search each receive one independent
request, 24 proposals, and the same 32 training pairs and 18 certified
specifications; only guided search receives contradiction witnesses.
Proposals are authored in Codex assistant turns using \texttt{gpt-6.1-sol}
with ultra reasoning, without external API calls. A fourth arm deterministically
enumerates 24 feature--rule combinations. Requests and candidates are
matched across assistant arms; unavailable tokens and differing validity
checks prevent a matched-compute claim. One batch per arm provides no
across-run generation variance.

We evaluate saved prefixes of 8, 16, and 24 proposals. Joint arms use the
same acyclicity and consistency gate $s\geq0.75$ and maximize complete
validation quality minus compiled-work penalty, $\lambda=0.002$.
Rule-only search retains valid rules regardless of consistency. The
minimum additive feature interface is a separate ablation; it does not
restrict the principal joint selection. Frozen controls vary the penalty,
feature limit, and inclusion of diagnostic validation workloads.

The selected guided, free, and rule-only programs are unchanged across
all three prefixes. Their training consistency is respectively 97.2\%,
94.4\%, and 11.1\%, demonstrating that strong schedule quality can coexist
with poor strict-requirement consistency. Deterministic enumeration attains
the highest validation quality (99.44\%) and 97.2\% consistency; this
comparison supplies no general LLM search advantage.

\subsection{Frozen Scheduling and Compiled Execution}
\label{sec:experiments:deployment}
Baselines include reward-first, reward/degree, reward/conflicting-weight,
the earlier structural joint program, and structural ratio. Local search
starts from their best schedule and applies improving exchanges including
one-to-two replacements for two seconds; HiGHS receives ten seconds per
context. On 102 acquired side requirements, the guided program reaches
88 saved boundaries, but next actions started at those boundaries escape
the pair in 98 cases. All four pair choices agree with the certificate.
Thus score consistency does not establish execution of the saved preference.

For a fixed neighborhood-edge-sum rule, 150 non-diagnostic validation
contexts compare interpreter and compiler with three interleaved repeats.
Every action trace agrees. Median interpreter/compiled timing ratios rise
from $1.10\times$ at 32 contacts to $2.44\times$ at 512; work ratios rise
from $3.19\times$ to $39.93\times$. Six C3-only 1,024-contact contexts yield
$4.45\times$ median timing speedup. Initialization, residual updates,
intersections, and scoring are all charged. These measurements concern
this rule; dense updates and unsupported expressions can retain substantial
cost. Neither acyclic evidence nor compiled execution guarantees greedy
optimality on unseen scheduling workloads.
