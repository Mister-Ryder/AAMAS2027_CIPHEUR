\section{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:problem}

Each candidate contact $v\in V$ specifies a satellite $\sigma_v$, ground
station $\gamma_v$, fixed interval $[s_v,e_v)$ with $s_v<e_v$, and
nonnegative reward $w_v$. Selecting it reserves the entire interval.
The configuration $\theta$ remains fixed throughout optimization; adaptation
compares separate static problems rather than online constraint changes.
Our main scheduling model gives every satellite and station capacity one.
For contacts $u,v$ ordered by start time, a common station causes a conflict
when $s_v<e_u+\tau_g$; a common satellite uses $s_v<e_u+\tau_s$.
The switching gaps $\tau_g,\tau_s$ are nonnegative configuration parameters.
Synthetic rewards are specified independently of contact duration.

Let $G_\theta=(V,E_\theta,w)$ contain exactly these incompatible pairs, and
write $w(I)=\sum_{v\in I}w_v$. Contact selection is maximum-weight independent
set optimization:
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\substack{I\subseteq V\\I\text{ independent in }G_\theta}}w(I).
 \label{eq:mwis}
\end{equation}
The formulation covers hard constraints expressible by pairwise conflicts.
Public graph instances exercise this MWIS problem without a satellite
physical interpretation. Historical C3 results retain their separate
inherited conflict predicates and serve as background, not validation of
the main synthetic model.

A boundary $B=(F,X)$ records permanent commitments $F$ and exclusions $X$.
It is admissible when $F,X\subseteq V$, $F\cap X=\varnothing$, and $F$ is
independent in $G_\theta$. With $N_\theta(F)$ the union of their neighbor
sets, the available contacts are
\begin{equation}
 R_\theta(B)=V\setminus\bigl(F\cup X\cup N_\theta(F)\bigr).
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
For $d\in R_\theta(B)$, its forced conditional completion value is
\begin{equation}
 V_\theta(d\mid B)=
 \max_{\substack{I\subseteq V\text{ independent in }G_\theta\\
                 F\cup\{d\}\subseteq I,\ I\cap X=\varnothing}}w(I).
 \label{eq:conditional-value}
\end{equation}
This value includes the fixed reward and every globally compatible
completion, rather than only contacts near the forced action.

An aligned intervention changes one resource-constraint parameter between
$\theta_0$ and $\theta_1$, preserving contact identities, resources,
intervals, rewards, and the declared model. The same boundary must be
admissible under both configurations. Competing actions $a,b$ must belong
to both available sets and be adjacent in both graphs, so each remains
individually feasible while their joint selection is forbidden. Changes
elsewhere can then reverse or preserve their conditional preference.
Offline evidence certifies such comparisons at this explicit boundary;
ties or unresolved bounds impose no strict ranking requirement.

The deployed object is a frozen deterministic typed priority program
$P=(\phi_P,h_P)$, assigning finite scores
$h_P(\phi_P(G_\theta,A,v))$ to $v\in A$ on a current active snapshot $A$.
It uses permitted graph and resource information through the typed library.
All program conditions share a classical anytime repair kernel, initializer,
patch-construction rules, feasibility checks, and budget conventions.
The priority changes local greedy proposals and search pivots; their
feasibility and bounding logic remain shared. Different improvements can
produce different subsequent incumbents and repair trajectories.

The kernel maintains an independent incumbent $I$ containing $F$ and
avoiding $X$. It can retract movable contacts in $I\setminus F$, while
permanent commitments remain protected. Each local replacement is compatible
with the temporarily retained exterior and is committed only after checking
global feasibility and strictly positive reward gain. Budget termination
retains the best feasible incumbent, which need not be maximal or globally
optimal. This bounded local classical search operates at deployment without
online LLM calls or full-residual certification-oracle calls.
Full-graph conditional labels do not certify preferences inside a restricted
repair patch with different temporary commitments. Rule agreement on the
certified snapshots and repair quality therefore require separate evaluation.
