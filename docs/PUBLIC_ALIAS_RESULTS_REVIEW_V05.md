# Independent review of the public alias diagnostic

This is an experiment report and implementation audit. It is not manuscript text.

The new diagnostic establishes genuine finite representation obstructions on public graph inputs. It does **not** establish that the frozen primary programme repairs those obstructions or that an LLM improves scheduling. The full benchmark and source-derived induced populations must be reported separately.

Its independent checks reproduce the original input-derived population and hash-ordered induced subsets, all nine base fields, first-eight lexicographic alias query order, graph identities, feasibility of every saved lower witness, connected-component partitions and cancellation, exact interval arithmetic, strict labels, primary neighbourhood covers, scores, and completion summaries. All **7,441 checks passed**. All 67 strict pairs have independently verified component upper bounds. Small components used a memoized exact include/exclude recurrence: 235 distinct components, 96,267 recurrence states. The four larger components supporting the two full-graph strict pairs used a separate complement-colouring decision proof: 717 states in total. It proves absence of an independent set larger than each saved upper bound; feasible saved witnesses establish matching lower values. No verifier ceiling was reached. This verification neither queried new action pairs nor changed a policy or selection rule.

The 192 saved unmatched-component occurrences larger than 32 vertices in non-strict rows retain the original executed-oracle upper-bound scope; their large upper frontier trees were not exported or independently replayed. Their query labels and arithmetic were checked, but the audit does not claim independent exactness for every unknown or tied full-graph row. The two full strict pairs were independently proved. The three verifier tests compare both recurrence and complement-colouring decisions with complete enumeration on 40 small randomly generated cases and verify explicit failure on a zero verification budget.

## Actual results

| Population | States | Assigned queries | Strict | Exact ties | Unknown | Quota shortfalls | Primary separates strict pairs | Primary agrees strictly |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Full unit DIMACS, at most 256 vertices | 13 | 104 | 2 | 28 | 74 | 0 | 1/2 | 0/2 |
| Unit source-derived induced graphs, at most 32 vertices | 48 | 220 | 65 | 155 | 0 | 164 | 21/65 | 13/65 |

There were no wall-cap interruptions. A missing eligible alias is a quota shortfall, not an unknown conditional value. The 164 shortfalls remain in the registered inventory. The induced strict pairs occur in 16 source-derived graphs. Of their 65 strict pairs, the frozen primary distinguishes 21, ranks 13 correctly, ranks 8 incorrectly, and ties on 44. Of the two full strict pairs, it ties on one and ranks the other incorrectly. These are comparisons of the two named actions; they are not whole-schedule quality or complete argmax measurements.

Each strict query compares existing competing vertices with exactly equal base vectors at the initial empty boundary. A certified strict preference therefore produces a quotient self-loop: **no deterministic pointwise scalar ranker of those nine fields can meet that strict requirement**. This is a finite interface impossibility result. Lexicographic quotas, equal-weight inputs and shared sources preclude a population incidence estimate. The full inputs are exact complements of public clique benchmark graphs; source-derived induced graphs are explicitly smaller algorithmic diagnostics. Neither is physical satellite contact data. One full 28-vertex source is also represented in the induced track, and the tracks must not be pooled as independent observations.

## The two full-graph obstructions

| Graph | Competing actions | Common degree | Forced completion values | Preferred | Primary cover feature | Primary outcome |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| `brock200_2` (200 vertices) | `0`, `118` | 108 | 10 versus 9 | `0` | 23 versus 23 | Scores tie |
| `san200_0.7_1` (200 vertices) | `100`, `38` | 55 | 30 versus 25 | `100` | 18 versus 17 | Wrong preference |

The nine fields are weight, duration, degree, conflicting-weight sum, maximum conflicting weight, compatible-weight sum, remaining count, station gap and satellite gap. For `brock200_2`, both actions have vector `(1,1,108,108,1,91,200,0,0)` in that order. For `san200_0.7_1`, both have `(1,1,55,55,1,144,200,0,0)`. The forced completion values include the unit-weight compared action. The independently proved unmatched component optima are 9 and 8 for the former, and 29 and 24 for the latter.

The primary is `weight/max(0.000001,weight,nc)`, where `nc` is the deterministic weighted clique partition of the competing neighbourhood. On `san200_0.7_1`, this feature distinguishes the actions but the score prefers `38`, opposite the certified optimum. Thus representation separation and score fitting are separate obligations. The old TRAIN declared-interface gate does not guarantee either on new graph populations. These results must not be recast as successful automatic repair or LLM benefit.

## Concise manuscript paragraph recommendation

> A separate fixed-program diagnostic tests whether feature aliasing also occurs on public graph inputs. We preregistered the initial state of 13 complete unit-weight DIMACS graphs with at most 256 vertices and 48 source-derived induced graphs with at most 32 vertices, querying the first eight competing edges with exactly equal nine-field base vectors. The complete graphs yield two strict conditional preferences, 28 exact ties and 74 unresolved comparisons; the induced track yields 65 strict preferences and 155 exact ties, with 164 missing-alias quota slots retained. Independent verification confirms every strict preference. Each strict alias makes pointwise ranking on the base interface impossible. The frozen primary separates one of two full-graph strict pairs and 21 of 65 induced strict pairs, but agrees with none and 13, respectively. This exposes both transferable representation obstructions and the limits of the current feature–rule pair; it does not measure physical scheduling incidence or establish an LLM effect.

For a compact paper table, use the two population rows above with columns `queries`, `strict/tie/unknown`, `feature split`, and `score agreement`. Put the exact full-pair details and source/audit receipts in an additional Markdown experiment report. The main paper should explain the distinction between interface sufficiency and scalar fitting, rather than display a positive-looking repair rate without its denominator and failures.
