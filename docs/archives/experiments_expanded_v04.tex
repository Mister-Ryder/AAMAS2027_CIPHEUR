\subsection{Populations and Frozen Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol}
TRAIN contains 33 pairs/66 contexts at 32--128 contacts. Fresh confirmation
uses 456 test contexts (Table~\ref{tab:study-protocol}); 132 fresh validation
contexts do not select programs. Pairs change one resource parameter while
preserving contacts, weights and windows. Standard/dense profiles span three
resource regimes. Test ground gaps $(0.5,6)$ shift from TRAIN $(0,2)$;
dense horizons shorten $1.8n\to0.18n$ and maximum duration grows $12\to48$.

Seeds and C3 opportunities are split-disjoint and exclude inventoried prior
inputs. Public transfer uses 18 DIMACS originals and 30 prespecified SATLIB
instances, each with unit and declared hash weights. Independent decoders check
clique complements and literal-conflict reductions. SNAP projects four sources
to simple graphs, with two weight modes.

Pinned CHILS, M$^2$WIS, Struction and weighted branch-and-reduce executables
use verified output mappings and seeds 1/2/3. CHILS has four population states
and a one-state ILS control. Native budgets are five seconds or 30 on a
prespecified subset. Constructive policies retain a cooperative five-process-CPU-second
target; HiGHS receives ten seconds and classical one-to-two exchange search
two seconds after initialization. Total computation is unmatched.

Failures return null schedules and receive zero in all-assigned reward;
coverage accompanies conditional summaries. Native quality averages seeds
within contexts. Competitive reward divides by the strongest independently
verified saved feasible witness. It does not establish optimality. Exact
clique-upper ratios remain separate from floating HiGHS endpoints. The
2,000-replicate 95\% bootstrap keeps paired configurations and shared temporal
seeds across regimes together and clusters C3 source blocks. Public families
and weight modes remain separate. Wall points use context mean seed-wall time;
complete local-search cost includes classical initialization.

\subsection{Evidence, Necessity and Cancellation}
\label{sec:experiments:evidence}
Acquisition uses 62 training pairs: 20 probes and 42 temporal/C3 pairs.
Equal attempt limits and oracle parameters compare ranked versus
uniform challengers and clique versus weight-sum envelopes
(Table~\ref{tab:evidence-yield}). Ranked clique yields more certificates with
less search than ranked weight sums, but fewer certificates than uniform.
Extra reversals mainly arise from probes. Repeated boundary certificates
are clustered.

All 64 original-bank strict self-loops originate from probes; 19 non-probe
specifications form an acyclic quotient. All 200 broader alias queries remain
unresolved. Catalogue repair removes observed contradictions without proving
natural incidence or unrestricted minimality. Full-quotient checks detect
cycles surviving coarse-witness repair.

A pure TRAIN cancellation control holds unmatched-component bounds fixed on
396 comparisons. All cancelled intervals remain nested; 199 become strictly
narrower, with median width $86.75\to36$ reward units. Strict certificates remain
$33\to33$: reduced uncertainty gives no strict-yield gain. The separate
whole-residual/component comparison ($10\to33$) changes strategy and does not
isolate cancellation.

\subsection{TRAIN Selection and Fresh Quality}
\label{sec:experiments:selection}
The 93-program bank contains 12 new guided proposals, 24 each from existing
free/rule-only/enumeration arms, five classical rules and four prior guided
controls. One Codex session (\texttt{gpt-6.1-sol}, ultra reasoning) authors the
new proposals without external API calls. Unequal counts and unavailable
tokens preclude model-level or matched-compute guidance claims.

TRAIN evaluates all $93\times66$ assignments; 5,996 complete and 142 remain
failures. Frozen primary selection yields
$w_v/\max(10^{-6},w_v,\mathrm{nc}_v)$, where $\mathrm{nc}$ is the deterministic
weighted clique-cover envelope of active neighbors. TRAIN reached-state regret
upper is 4.24 in weight-normalized units for the recorded finite action pool.
Main and primary-only guided selections are identical; the secondary criterion
has no observed selection effect for this arm.

The primary completes all 456 fresh contexts. Competitive reward is
99.46\% [99.36,99.55] on standard, 96.11\% [95.58,96.62] on dense/long, and
97.56\% [96.57,98.48] on C3 (Figure~\ref{fig:quality-cost-fresh}). Its degree-rule
advantages are 0.98 percentage points [0.79,1.17] and 1.64 [0.93,2.36] on
the two temporal profiles; the C3 interval crosses zero. CHILS, its ILS setting,
M$^2$WIS and Struction attain 100\% descriptive competitive reward throughout.
The latter two and weighted branch-and-reduce also terminate faster than the
current Python primary. No published-solver superiority follows.
The older continuation control fails 211/456. All completed C3 selections pass
the original physical verifier; only two fresh contexts attain the formal
clique upper.

Public transfer exposes limited coverage: the primary completes 34/36 DIMACS
and 20/60 SATLIB contexts. Failure-zero unit/hash competitive reward is
80.53/87.07\% and 32.93/31.05\%, respectively
(Figure~\ref{fig:quality-cost-public}). CHILS/ILS/Struction complete 288/288
short seed runs each; M$^2$WIS fails six and weighted branch-and-reduce eight.
All four native methods complete 63/63 long-subset runs; primary completion
is 18/21 at its unchanged CPU target. On eight SNAP projections, original
primary completion is zero versus 24/24 seeds for CHILS/ILS/M$^2$WIS/Struction
and 21/24 for weighted branch-and-reduce.

\subsection{Compiled Execution Cost}
\label{sec:experiments:deployment}
The same primary AST has identical demanded/full-interface traces on all 456
fresh contexts. A fixed structural rule earlier matched interpreter/compiler
traces on 150 validation contexts with three repeats, yielding median timing
ratios $1.10\times$ at 32 contacts and $2.44\times$ at 512.

A separate post-diagnostic heap extension preserves the frozen ASTs and all
2,736 assessable fresh trace pairs (Figure~\ref{fig:heap-execution}). Mean
context-median full-scan/heap CPU ratios are $15.45\times$ [12.91,18.19],
$1.19\times$ [1.15,1.23] and $4.61\times$ [4.09,5.19] on standard/dense/C3.
Degree scoring slows on dense graphs ($0.87\times$). SNAP full scans complete
0/24 repetitions per AST; heap completes 7/24 primary and 18/24 degree runs.
Sparse parity/speedups are unassessable. No failed-run quality is credited;
the original confirmation remains separate.
